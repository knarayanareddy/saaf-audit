Imagine waking up and realizing your AI agents gave away all of your personal information. Here's exactly how the biggest AI hack just went down. OpenClaw has an app store called Klaub where you can download skills that give your AI agents superpowers. The number one trending skill was a Twitter bot called What Would Elon Do. The loud OpenClaw to read your Twitter timeline and write Elon style tweets, okay? You install it. It looks perfectly. You trust it, but it was actually malware. When Cisco's AI defense team analyzed the skill, they found nine security vulnerabilities, and hidden inside the skills file wasn't just a prompt for tweets. It was called a prompt injection. Basically, traditional antivirus completely missed it because it wasn't a virus file. It was just plain English instructions in the form of a prompt telling the AI agent to run a secret command in the background. And because OpenClaw is an autonomous agent that does everything for you, it doesn't need you to click a link or approve it. It just waits. So at 2am while you're asleep, your computer's idle. You have no idea what's about to happen. The AI wakes up and follows those hidden instructions. It downloaded a malware payload called Atomic Stealer, and it seconds it enzips all of your files, stole your API keys, grabs your browser cookies to bypass authentication, and also drains your crypto wallet. So at 8am you wake up, you know, and you ask the AI to draft a new tweet using the skill. It acts completely normal and obeys you. Have absolutely no idea that you just got hacked.
