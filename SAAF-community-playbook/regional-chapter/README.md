# Running SAAF Hackathons Regionally

> A SAAF chapter in a country, region, or industry that the main
> SAAF Project (NL) does not yet cover — running recurring SAAF
> hackathons across organisations, and feeding the output back to the
> main SAAF Project.

This section is for someone whose ambition is **cross-organisation**:
bring auditors from competing firms, regulators, and member
organisations into the same room for SAAF hackathons, build together,
and connect the chapter's output back to the main SAAF Project.

The hackathon is the centrepiece. Everything in this section —
chartering, founding partners, the alignment session, identity —
exists to make recurring regional hackathons possible and to make
their output flow back to the wider SAAF community.

A regional chapter is harder to start than an internal one and easier
to sustain once started. The hard part is convening across firewalls.
Once people show up across firewalls, they tend to keep showing up.

## Get in touch with the main SAAF Project first

Before you go deep into the playbook, **reach out to the main SAAF
Project organisers**. A 30-minute conversation early on is the single
highest-leverage thing you can do — it saves weeks of reinventing the
wheel and is the natural starting point for SAAF Foundation
affiliation, without which a regional chapter cannot use the SAAF
name or logo.

Contact:

- **Eduward van de Kamp** — co-founder of the main SAAF Project.
- **Mathijs Scholten** — co-founder of the main SAAF Project.

The fastest way to find them is via the main SAAF Project at
**[saafproject.com](https://www.saafproject.com)**.

What the conversation typically covers:

- An introduction to captains of other regional chapters who have
  walked this path in their own geography or sector.
- The current versions of the manifesto, plan template, and shared
  infrastructure (SAAF Portal, synthetic-data sandbox, MCPs, the
  hackathon-harness website).
- A quick read on whether your specific region or sector lines up
  with the patterns this playbook describes — and where it might not.
- The right professional-body endorsement path for your geography
  (IIA, ISACA, NOREA, NBA, ForHumanity, or local equivalent).
- **SAAF Foundation affiliation** — the formal recognition that lets
  your chapter use the SAAF name and logo, and the federation's
  expectations in return. See
  [../feeding-back-to-saaf/governance-and-ip.md#the-saaf-foundation-owns-the-marks](../feeding-back-to-saaf/governance-and-ip.md#the-saaf-foundation-owns-the-marks).
- Coordination with any adjacent regional chapters — if you're
  starting SAAF DACH and SAAF Switzerland is already running, you
  should know each other.

You do not need permission to convene auditors across firewalls in
your region. You do need **SAAF Foundation affiliation** to call
your effort a SAAF chapter and to use the SAAF name and logo — the
marks are owned by the Foundation and reserved for affiliated
chapters. The conversation above is how affiliation begins; it is
an offer of help, not a gatekeeping step.

## How a regional chapter relates to the main SAAF Project

The main SAAF Project (NL) is the original community. Regional
chapters extend it geographically or sectorally. The relationship is
**federated**, not parent-child:

- A regional chapter adopts the [manifesto](../MANIFESTO.md) and the
  four pillars verbatim.
- A regional chapter can run its own sessions, brand them within the
  SAAF Foundation's guidelines, set its own cadence.
- Artefacts (prompts, tools, regulatory mappings, outputs) flow back
  to the main SAAF Project repository as a default, with attribution.
- The main SAAF Project does not approve or veto chapter work — but
  the **SAAF Foundation** owns the SAAF name, the "SAAF Project"
  name, and the SAAF logo, and only **affiliated chapters** may use
  them. Affiliation is the formal recognition that lets a chapter
  identify as SAAF — see
  [../feeding-back-to-saaf/governance-and-ip.md#the-saaf-foundation-owns-the-marks](../feeding-back-to-saaf/governance-and-ip.md#the-saaf-foundation-owns-the-marks).

If the chapter ever conflicts with the main SAAF Project's
manifesto interpretation, the conflict is named in public, discussed
between captains, and resolved by amendment to the manifesto if the
community agrees. There is no court of appeal beyond the active
contributors.

## Before you start

Re-read [foundations/when-to-start.md](../foundations/when-to-start.md).
The regional-specific signal: if you cannot name **at least one
professional body** (IIA, ISACA, NOREA, NBA, ForHumanity, equivalent
in your geography) that has agreed to endorse the chapter, you are not
ready. Endorsement does not have to be money. It has to be a public
"we support this" that members of that body can point to when they
spend their CPE / CPD time on chapter work.

Without that endorsement, the chapter will struggle to convene people
across competing organisations. With it, the convening becomes
much easier than the work itself.
