# Identity And Positioning

How the chapter looks, sounds, and feels to the outside world — and
how to keep that consistent over time.

This page is intentionally short. A regional chapter's identity is not
a brand exercise; it is the public expression of the manifesto. If
you spend more than a couple of weeks on it, you are over-thinking it.

---

## The Public Face

Three things, minimum:

1. **A one-page chapter site or page**, hosted somewhere stable
   (member organisation domain, professional body domain, dedicated
   subdomain). Contains: chapter name, vision paragraph, manifesto
   link, founding partners, endorsing body, captain's contact, next
   session date, link to the main SAAF Project.

2. **A communication channel** — Slack, Teams, or LinkedIn group, one
   only. Where members talk to each other between sessions.

3. **A repository** for plans, prompts, and recaps. Either in the
   main SAAF Project's GitHub organisation (preferred — see
   [feeding-back-to-saaf/](../feeding-back-to-saaf/)) or a chapter
   repository that mirrors the four-pillar structure.

You do not need a logo, a tagline, a Twitter account, a podcast, or a
newsletter. You may end up with some of those eventually. None of
them are pre-conditions for a working chapter.

## Naming Conventions

Recap from [chartering-a-chapter.md](chartering-a-chapter.md): the
chapter must qualify the SAAF name. *SAAF Belgium*, *SAAF Public
Sector*, *SAAF DACH* — yes. *SAAF* alone — no. And only
**Foundation-affiliated** chapters may use any form of the SAAF name
or logo at all.

Use the qualified name everywhere. In email signatures, on the chapter
site, in announcements, in recaps. Consistency makes the federation
model legible to outsiders.

The **SAAF logo** is owned by the SAAF Foundation; affiliated
chapters use it under the Foundation's brand guidelines (request the
current version from the main project organisers). The visual
identity (colours, typography) can take cues from the main SAAF
Project's site at https://www.saafproject.com — distinct enough to
read as the chapter, similar enough to read as part of the
federation. Avoid heavy rebranding that hides the federation, and
never modify the SAAF logo without explicit Foundation approval.

## How To Talk About The Chapter

The chapter's positioning, in one paragraph that the captain can
deliver from memory:

> *"[Chapter name] is a regional chapter of the SAAF Project, the
> Shared Audit Agents Framework. We are auditors, Professional Practices leads,
> analysts, and engineers from [N] organisations across [region], who
> meet [N] times a year to build AI audit agents together. Our work
> is open, vendor-agnostic, and grounded in real audit problems. We
> federate with the main SAAF Project in the Netherlands; the
> manifesto is shared."*

Adjust the numbers and geography. Keep the vendor-agnostic and
manifesto bits. Resist the temptation to add more.

If a journalist or a vendor asks you for a definition longer than
that paragraph, you have already given them the right one.

## Vendor Relationships, In Public

The chapter will receive vendor interest. Some of it generous, some
of it transactional, some of it both. The public-facing rules:

- **Sponsorship is acknowledged, not advertised.** Sponsors get a
  thank-you line in the recap, a logo on the session-day signage, and
  attendance for their people. They do not get logo placement on
  every session, name in the chapter title, or "powered by" framing.

- **No exclusive sessions.** Even if a vendor is the host and the
  caterer, other vendors are welcome to attend, and other vendors'
  products can be demoed. The host's role is logistical, not
  curatorial.

- **No co-marketing without explicit sign-off.** A vendor cannot
  publish a case study about the chapter without the captain's
  written approval. The captain's default answer is "no" unless the
  case study is reviewed by the founding partners.

- **Vendor talks are real talks, not pitches.** Acceptable: a vendor's
  research team presenting on agent-evaluation methodology with no
  product mention. Not acceptable: a "thought leadership" talk that
  reduces to "and our product does this." If the talk would not
  survive the removal of the product mention, decline it.

When in doubt, the litmus test: *would a competing vendor be willing
to attend the next session?* If the answer is "probably not," the
chapter has tipped.

## When Things Go Public

At some point a journalist, a podcast, or an industry publication will
write about the chapter. Anticipate this; do not be surprised by it.

Prepare:

- A press kit page with the vision paragraph, the manifesto, founding
  partners, endorsing body, captain's contact, and a high-resolution
  version of any logo you use.
- A list of two or three contributors who are comfortable being
  quoted. Always offer the press a contributor besides the captain;
  the chapter is not one person.
- A clear "what to credit" instruction: name the chapter, name
  contributors with their consent and pseudonyms preserved if
  requested, name the federation with the main SAAF Project.

Do *not* reveal:

- Real audit data, real client names, or real findings.
- The internal stack of any member organisation.
- Anything a member organisation has not given consent to share.

The chapter's interesting story is *the format that works*, not any
specific organisation's audit work. Keep the story on the format and
the artefacts.

## Languages

If the chapter operates in a region whose working language is not
English, sessions can run in the local language. Recap documents and
artefacts contributed back to the main SAAF Project repository
are in English (see
[foundations/what-saaf-is.md](../foundations/what-saaf-is.md)).

Bilingual chapter sites are fine. The manifesto is available in
English; chapters are welcome to translate it for local use, but the
English version remains the canonical one for federation purposes.

---

## Drift

Identity drift happens slowly. The chapter starts with a clear vision
paragraph, and three years later the website is a sprawling set of
service pages and the chapter has somehow become a consultancy in
disguise. Defend against this by:

- Reviewing the chapter site once a year against the manifesto.
- Asking, at every captain rotation, whether the public positioning
  still matches the work.
- Cutting any page that has not been updated in twelve months and
  cannot be quickly justified.

The chapter's identity is at its strongest when its public face is
small, clear, and current. Big and stale is worse than small and live.
