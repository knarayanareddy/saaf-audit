# Chartering A Regional Chapter

The charter is the document that says, in writing, what the chapter is,
what it is not, who runs it, and how it relates to everything else.
It is short — one to three pages. It is read at every captain rotation
and reviewed annually.

This page covers the substantive decisions that go into the charter.
The template itself lives at
[appendix/templates/chapter-charter.md](../appendix/templates/chapter-charter.md).

---

## The Naming Decision

The **SAAF name**, the **"SAAF Project"** name, the **SAAF logo**,
and related marks are owned by the **SAAF Foundation**. They may
only be used by affiliated SAAF chapters. Before settling on a name
for the chapter, secure Foundation affiliation — see
[../feeding-back-to-saaf/governance-and-ip.md#the-saaf-foundation-owns-the-marks](../feeding-back-to-saaf/governance-and-ip.md#the-saaf-foundation-owns-the-marks).

The unqualified word **"SAAF"** is reserved for the main SAAF Project
(NL). A regional chapter must qualify the name.

**Acceptable forms** (subject to Foundation affiliation):

- *SAAF Belgium*, *SAAF UK*, *SAAF DACH*
- *SAAF Public Sector*, *SAAF Financial Services*, *SAAF Healthcare*
- *SAAF NYC*, *SAAF Singapore*

**Not acceptable:**

- "SAAF" alone, without qualifier.
- Names that imply ownership or hierarchy ("SAAF Global," "SAAF HQ",
  "SAAF International").
- Vendor-coupled names ("SAAF Powered By [vendor]").
- Any SAAF-bearing name used by a community that is **not affiliated
  with the SAAF Foundation**. A non-affiliated community can be a
  great community; it just needs a different name.

The chapter's logo, website, and public materials should clearly
position the chapter as a regional/sectoral instance, with a visible
link to the main SAAF Project at https://www.saafproject.com, and
follow the SAAF Foundation's brand guidelines (request the current
version from the main project organisers).

The naming convention exists for one reason: when a member organisation
hears "SAAF Belgium," they know it is a real, **Foundation-affiliated**
chapter, federated with the main project, following the manifesto.
That trust is worth defending.

## Governance

Regional chapters are deliberately lightweight in governance. The
minimum:

| Role | Count | Term |
|---|---|---|
| Captain | 1 | 12–24 months, named successor before rotation |
| Co-captain | 1 | Same term as captain or staggered |
| Founding partners | 2–4 organisations | Indefinite — see below |
| Endorsing professional body | 1+ | Annual reaffirmation |

### Captain

One named human. Holds the chapter. Defends the manifesto. Writes the
quarterly recap. Hands over cleanly when the term ends. Captains can
serve consecutive terms only with explicit consent of the founding
partners and the endorsing body.

### Co-captain

Shadow of the captain. Runs sessions when the captain is out. The
co-captain is the planned successor if the captain steps down. Having
a co-captain is non-negotiable; chapters with a single point of
failure have a half-life of about 18 months.

### Founding partners

The 2–4 organisations whose people made the chapter exist. Their role
is *not* to vote on chapter decisions; their role is to be the
backbone of attendance and contribution. They commit to:

- Sending at least one builder to every session for the first year.
- Hosting at least one session in the first 18 months (rotation).
- Putting one person on the founding-team roster (see
  [founding-team-and-partners.md](founding-team-and-partners.md)).

After the first year, the founding-partner status is honorary. New
member organisations are not "lesser" than founding partners — they
are simply newer.

### Endorsing professional body

At least one body (IIA, ISACA, NOREA, NBA, equivalent) endorses the
chapter publicly. The endorsement is renewed annually, in writing.
The body does not run the chapter; it confirms that the chapter is
operating in good faith and aligned with the profession.

If the endorsing body asks for control of the chapter — agenda
approval, speaker approval, branding control — politely decline. The
endorsement model is about legitimacy, not authority.

## IP And Sharing Defaults

Regional chapters publish artefacts back to the main SAAF Project
repository under the same license the national project uses. As of
this writing, the SAAF Project artefacts are open and shared; the
chapter's contributions inherit that.

The defaults, written into the charter:

| Artefact type | Default | Exception |
|---|---|---|
| Prompts | Public, contributed back | Trade-secret-grade prompts may stay internal — but document the *category* of prompt publicly. |
| Code (scripts, MCPs) | Public, contributed back | Code that wraps a paid API may be public; the credentials never are. |
| Control mappings | Public, contributed back | Rare exceptions for proprietary internal frameworks. |
| Output schemas / templates | Public, contributed back | None — schemas should always be public. |
| Real audit data | **Never shared, never stored in the chapter's spaces.** | None. |
| Synthetic data | Public, contributed back | None. |
| Recap documents | Public to chapter members; selected lessons public to community | None — chapter members should always see their own recap. |

Anything more restrictive than this is fine for an *internal* chapter
inside a single organisation, but a *regional* chapter that hides its
artefacts is not really a SAAF chapter.

## Funding

Regional chapters typically run on a mix of:

- **In-kind venue and catering** from rotating member-organisation
  hosts.
- **Sponsor contribution** from vendors, professional bodies, or
  member organisations — pooled, not exclusive.
- **Member organisation contributions** — small per-organisation
  annual fee or no fee, depending on the chapter.
- **Volunteered time** from the captain and champions.

What you should *not* do:

- Take money in exchange for branding or agenda control.
- Take money from a single vendor that exceeds the combined
  contribution of all other sponsors. (Rough threshold: no single
  sponsor should be more than 40% of total funding.)
- Charge participants to attend regular sessions. (Optional advanced
  workshops or external events are different.)

If the chapter cannot be run on this funding model, it probably needs
to be smaller, less frequent, or backed more heavily by a
professional body. A chapter forced to compromise on funding will
quietly compromise on neutrality.

## Decision-Making

Most decisions inside a chapter are made by the captain in
consultation with the co-captain and any track leads. The charter
should name the small set of decisions that require broader
consultation:

- Adoption or amendment of the chapter's interpretation of the
  manifesto.
- Captain rotation.
- Disposition of any single sponsor over the 40% threshold.
- A change in cadence (e.g. quarterly to monthly).
- A change in geography or scope (e.g. SAAF Belgium expanding to
  SAAF Benelux).

For these decisions, the captain consults the founding partners and
the endorsing body. There is no formal voting structure unless the
chapter chooses to add one — and most should not. Consensus among
active contributors is the norm.

## Relationship To The Main SAAF Project And The SAAF Foundation

The main SAAF Project does not control regional chapters. The **SAAF
Foundation** owns the marks. Together, they maintain three things:

1. **The manifesto.** Chapters adopt it as is. Amendments are
   discussed in the open with the main project organisers.
2. **The SAAF marks (name, "SAAF Project," logo).** Owned by the
   SAAF Foundation. Chapters must qualify their names and use the
   marks under the Foundation's brand guidelines; affiliation is the
   formal grant of permission.
3. **The repository structure.** Chapters that contribute back use
   the four pillars and the existing folder layout. Local
   extensions are fine; renaming the pillars is not.

In return, the main project (in coordination with the Foundation)
commits to:

- Listing the affiliated regional chapter on the SAAF Project website.
- Including a chapter-rotation slot in the main project's annual
  organiser sync.
- Making main-project artefacts available to the chapter on the same
  terms as to any member.

The relationship is described in writing in the chapter's charter and
acknowledged by the national project organisers. The acknowledgment is
not approval — chapters do not need approval to exist — it is a
declaration that both sides understand the federation model.

## A Charter Length Test

If the charter is longer than three pages, you have made it too
complicated. Either you are over-specifying the governance, or you are
trying to solve future arguments in advance. Don't. Write less; amend
when you have to.

The minimum useful charter:

1. Vision (one paragraph).
2. Scope and naming.
3. Governance (captain, co-captain, founding partners, endorsing body).
4. IP and sharing defaults.
5. Funding model.
6. Decisions requiring broader consultation.
7. Relationship to the main SAAF Project.
8. Manifesto adoption (one line: "We adopt the SAAF Community
   Manifesto as published at [URL]").
9. Year-one success criteria.
10. Signatories: captain, co-captain, founding-partner representatives,
    endorsing-body representative.

Use the [chapter charter template](../appendix/templates/chapter-charter.md)
as a starting point. Edit ruthlessly.
