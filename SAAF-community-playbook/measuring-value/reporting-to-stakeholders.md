# Reporting To Stakeholders

Three reporting cadences, three different documents. Each has a
specific audience and a specific job. Mixing them — one document
trying to do all three — produces a document that does none well.

---

## The Three Reports

| Report | Cadence | Audience | Purpose | Length |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **5-line update** | Monthly | Executive sponsor / endorsing body lead | Keep them informed, surface decisions needed | 5 lines |
| **Quarterly readout** | Quarterly | Sponsor + their peers + chapter members | Show outcomes, update on the four pillars | 1 page |
| **Annual case** | Annually | Sponsor + budget holders | Make the case for the next year's investment | 3 pages |

These nest: the quarterly readouts feed the annual case; the monthly
updates feed the quarterly readouts. Don't write each one from
scratch.

---

## The 5-Line Update

A monthly email. Five lines. Sent on a fixed day each month so the
recipient learns to expect it.

```
Subject: SAAF [Chapter Name] — [Month] update

1. Shipped: [single most important artefact or progress this month]
2. In flight: [the next thing, with target session]
3. Blocked: [the thing that is stuck and why]
4. Decision needed from you: [if any — explicit ask or "none this month"]
5. Next session: [date, venue]
```

That's the whole thing. No preamble, no wrap-up.

### Why five lines

Executives have inboxes. The chapter is one of dozens of things they
track. The five-line format respects their time and signals that the
chapter is being run by someone who understands prioritisation.

The discipline of writing five lines forces clarity. If you cannot
say what shipped this month in one line, what shipped is unclear in
your own head.

### What goes in each line

**Line 1 (Shipped).** Specific. *"Vendor risk pre-screen agent
demoed at session 4; entering Check stage at [Organisation A] in
March."* Not *"Continued work on vendor risk."*

**Line 2 (In flight).** What's happening before the next month's
update. The next session, a pilot launch, a specific plan target.

**Line 3 (Blocked).** Honest. If nothing is blocked, write *"Nothing
this month."* Avoid pretending blockers don't exist.

**Line 4 (Decision needed).** The most important line. Most months
the answer is *"None this month."* When there is one, name it
specifically: budget approval, an introduction, a sign-off. Make it
easy for the executive to say yes by email.

**Line 5 (Next session).** Date and venue. Even if unchanged from
last month. The recipient should always know the next concrete
moment.

### What does not belong

- Detailed metrics (those are in the quarterly readout).
- Activity counts.
- Storytelling about how a session went.
- Thanks to participants (those are in the recap, not the executive
  update).

If you find yourself writing more than five lines, write the recap
or the quarterly readout instead, and link to it.

## The Quarterly Readout

A one-page document. Read in five minutes. Anchored in the four value
pillars. Distributed to:

- The executive sponsor and their direct reports.
- The endorsing body's senior contact.
- The chapter's founding partners.
- Other captains in the federation (when the readout is interesting
  to them).

### Default structure

```markdown
# [Chapter Name] — [Quarter] Readout

## Summary
[Two or three sentences: what the chapter shipped, what the
executive most needs to know, what's next.]

## Pillar 1 — Audit hours returned
[One paragraph, with at least one quantitative claim and the
assumption it depends on.]

## Pillar 2 — Coverage and confidence
[One paragraph, with a specific population or domain expanded.]

## Pillar 3 — Risk surfaced earlier
[One paragraph, with a specific finding or pattern. If nothing this
quarter, say so explicitly: "No Pillar 3 results this quarter."]

## Pillar 4 — Capability and resilience
[One paragraph, with a specific capability gain or roster of new
contributors.]

## Plan portfolio status
[A small table: plans by PDCA stage, with the trend vs last
quarter.]

## What we got wrong
[Three to five sentences. The most important section. Don't skip it.]

## Next quarter
[What's planned. Two or three concrete commitments.]
```

### "What we got wrong"

This section is the most important and the most often skipped.
Executives are unmoved by readouts where everything is positive.
They are deeply moved — and earn enduring trust — by readouts that
honestly say what didn't work.

What goes here:

- A pilot that failed.
- A plan that didn't progress despite resources.
- A retention drop in champions.
- A measurement claim from last quarter that, on review, was wrong.

Write it directly. *"We claimed last quarter that the agent saved 40
hours per audit. On live data, the figure was closer to 12. The
synthetic test set didn't include three edge cases that dominate
real workload."* That paragraph builds more sponsor trust than ten
positive findings.

### What does not belong

- Every plan in the portfolio. The plan-by-plan view goes on the
  chapter site, not in the readout.
- Vendor mentions, except as factual notation ("we used [X] API for
  the prototype").
- Photos. The readout is a decision document.
- Quotes from participants. Those go in the recap.

## The Annual Case

Three pages. Once a year. Used to argue for the next year's budget,
the next year's air cover, the next year's commitment from the
endorsing body.

### Default structure

```markdown
# [Chapter Name] — Year [N] Annual Case

## What we set out to do this year
[One paragraph, with the year-start commitments quoted directly.]

## What we accomplished
[Half a page. Anchored in the four pillars. Specific, with numbers
where possible. No vanity metrics.]

## What we got wrong
[Half a page. Honest, specific, with what's been learned.]

## Where the chapter is now
[A one-paragraph state-of-the-chapter assessment: active members,
champion bench depth, plan portfolio shape, momentum.]

## What we propose for next year
[The forward-looking section. One paragraph on theme. A small table
of the year's planned sessions. The budget ask, with line items.
Specific commitments back to the sponsor and the endorsing body.]

## What would cause us to recommend pausing or restructuring
[One paragraph. Honest pre-mortem of conditions under which the
chapter should not continue in its current form. Sponsors trust
chapters that name the conditions for their own discontinuation.]

## Asks
[Specific, named asks. Budget line items. Introductions needed.
Sign-offs requested.]
```

The "what would cause us to pause" section is unusual and worth
highlighting. Most internal initiatives never tell their sponsor
what would cause them to recommend their own end. SAAF's annual case
should — it is the strongest signal of seriousness.

### Where the annual case differs from the quarterly readout

The annual case is forward-looking. The quarterly readout is
backward-looking with a forward-pointer. The case asks for
investment; the readout reports against existing investment.

Don't substitute one for the other. Some chapters quietly let the
annual case become a four-quarter readout. That misses the
investment-decision job entirely.

---

## Tone

Plain, professional, honest. Specific names where consent is given.
Numbers with their assumptions. Direct language about what worked
and what didn't.

Avoid:

- Marketing voice. *"Game-changing," "transformative," "next-level"*
  belong in vendor pitches.
- Hedging. *"It might be the case that perhaps the agent could
  potentially save..."* — write the claim or don't.
- Mock humility. *"Our humble little chapter..."* — undermines
  itself.
- Vendor-tinged language. *"AI-powered," "best-in-class," "industry-
  leading"* — the chapter is not a product.

The voice is auditor-to-auditor. Careful, evidenced, willing to say
"we don't know yet."

---

## Distribution Discipline

Each report goes to a specific list. Don't broaden distribution
without thought.

- The 5-line update goes to *one or two* recipients (sponsor; maybe
  one direct report). Forwarded only with the captain's consent.
- The quarterly readout goes to a defined list of about a dozen
  people. The captain knows every name on the list.
- The annual case goes to fewer people, more carefully. It is a
  decision document, not a bulletin.

Resist requests to "add me to the distribution." Each addition
slightly changes what the captain feels they can write. A widely-
distributed update becomes a guarded update; a guarded update is a
useless update.

If someone wants to be informed at a higher cadence, offer them a
direct one-on-one with the captain instead. That is more useful for
both sides.
