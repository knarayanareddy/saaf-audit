# Operating Rhythm

When the chapter looks at what. The rhythm of measurement and
reporting, designed so it doesn't eat the chapter's time.

The discipline: **high-quality signal matters more than comprehensive
dashboards.** A chapter that spends two days a quarter on measurement
is mismeasuring its own time. Aim for half a day.

---

## The Default Rhythm

| Cadence | What | Owner | Time required |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weekly | Channel triage; quick check on plan portfolio status | Captain | 30 min |
| Per session | Update PDCA status of every active plan in the recap | Recap writer + captain | included in recap time |
| Monthly | 5-line update to sponsor | Captain | 30 min |
| Quarterly | Quarterly readout (one page); plan portfolio review | Captain + co-captain | 4 hours total |
| Annually | Annual case (three pages); roll-call list; charter review | Captain + founding team | 1.5 days |

Total: roughly half a day a month, with a half-day spike each quarter
and a 1.5-day spike annually.

If your measurement work consistently exceeds this, you have:

- Too many metrics (cut to the four pillars).
- Too long a reporting document (compress).
- Too wide a distribution (narrow it).
- Too many ad-hoc one-off reports (decline most; set the rhythm).

---

## The Per-Session Beat

Every session produces:

1. The recap, including a current PDCA status for every plan that
   moved.
2. An update to the plan portfolio (the table of plans by PDCA
   stage).
3. A note in the captain's running log of what worked and what
   didn't.

That third item is private to the captain — a paragraph or three on
the day. Re-read before the next session. It is the cheapest source
of session-over-session improvement available.

---

## The Monthly Beat

The 5-line update.

The captain sets a recurring calendar block: 30 minutes, the first
Tuesday of every month, at the same time. Same template each month.
Send before lunch.

If the captain is travelling or unavailable, the co-captain sends
it. The update goes regardless. A skipped month is a stronger signal
of trouble than a routine month with thin content.

---

## The Quarterly Beat

The quarterly readout. Roughly four hours of work spread across the
last week of the quarter.

The mechanic:

1. Day -7: captain pulls together the four-pillar evidence from the
   last three sessions' recaps.
2. Day -5: captain drafts the readout.
3. Day -3: co-captain reviews. At least one substantive change
   (sharpening a number, calling out a missing context).
4. Day -1: captain sends to the distribution list.

The four-hour figure assumes the quarter's evidence has been
collected as it accumulated. A captain who tries to gather a quarter
of evidence in the final week will spend twelve hours, not four.
Discipline is in the per-session beat.

---

## The Plan Portfolio Review

Once a quarter, the captain and one or two track leads sit down with
the full plan portfolio and ask:

1. *Which plans moved this quarter?*
2. *Which plans are stuck — for how long, and why?*
3. *Which plans should be retired, merged, or re-scoped?*
4. *Which plans should be carried forward to the next quarter as
   priorities?*

The output is an updated plan portfolio document. The summary goes
into the quarterly readout under "Plan portfolio status." The
captain announces stuck-plan re-scoping or retirement in the next
session's recap.

This review is the chapter's most important hygiene activity. A plan
portfolio that is never pruned grows by 30% a year and becomes
unreadable by year three. A pruned portfolio stays useful.

---

## The Annual Beat

Once a year, three deliverables:

1. **The annual case** (three pages).
2. **The roll-call list** (every contributor, with consent).
3. **The charter review** (does the charter still describe the
   chapter, or has reality drifted?).

Total time: 1.5 days, ideally split across a week. The annual case
takes the most time and benefits from sleeping on the draft.

This is also the right moment for:

- Captain rotation review (have we been in role 12+ months? Time to
  start succession planning?).
- Cadence review (is quarterly still the right beat, or should we
  consider a change?).
- Funding model review (are we still vendor-neutral by the criteria
  in the charter?).

These reviews don't always produce changes. They produce the
*decision* that no change is needed, which is a different and
healthier thing than drift.

## Avoiding Measurement Bloat

The thing that quietly destroys the rhythm is **scope creep on
metrics**. A new sponsor asks for a new metric. A founding partner
requests a per-organisation breakdown. A vendor asks for an
attribution dashboard.

The captain's discipline:

- *"What decision will this metric inform?"* — if there isn't one,
  decline.
- *"Will adding this metric change what the chapter does?"* — if
  yes, the chapter has been redirected; if no, decline.
- *"Can we provide this once, on request, instead of routinely?"* —
  often the right answer for one-off curiosity.

Sponsor trust is built more by the chapter saying *"we don't track
that, here's why"* than by saying yes to every metric request. The
discipline is to **focus on a small number of value pillars and apply
them consistently** — consistently *across reports*, not increasingly
*per report*.

## When To Step Up The Rhythm

Most chapters should be cautious about stepping up. Conditions
where it is appropriate:

- The chapter has had a major shift (new sponsor, new captain, new
  strategic direction). Add monthly readouts for one quarter to
  rebuild trust, then revert.
- A specific plan is in active Check stage at multiple
  organisations. Weekly status notes to the involved captains and
  audit teams, for the duration of the pilot only.
- The chapter is preparing the annual case. The captain may run a
  daily 30-minute writing block for two weeks.

In all cases, the heightened rhythm is **time-boxed**. It is a
campaign, not a new normal. After the campaign, revert.

---

## When To Step Down The Rhythm

If the chapter is running smoothly and the sponsor is responsive at
quarterly cadence, you can sometimes drop the monthly 5-line update
to bi-monthly. The signals that this is OK:

- Sponsor has been stable for over a year.
- Sponsor proactively asks about the chapter (rather than reading
  passively).
- The quarterly readout consistently lands well.
- The chapter has not had a major incident in 12+ months.

Don't drop monthly cadence at a chapter's first year. The monthly
beat earns the right to bi-monthly later.

## A Note On Tools

You do not need a measurement platform. The default toolkit:

- A spreadsheet of plans by PDCA stage (one tab per quarter, or one
  master with a status column).
- A folder of recaps (chapter site or repo).
- A folder of readouts (separate from the public recap folder).
- A captain's private running log (any text file).

Adding tooling beyond this — a Notion database, a metabase
dashboard, a custom GitHub Action that scrapes recaps — can be
useful for mature chapters and almost always premature for new
ones. Earn the tooling.

The main SAAF Project has a "Hackathon Harness" website that
auto-generates plan dashboards from markdown files. Chapters that
contribute their plans to the SAAF GitHub organisation get this for
free; chapters running their own infrastructure should consider
whether they need to replicate it.
