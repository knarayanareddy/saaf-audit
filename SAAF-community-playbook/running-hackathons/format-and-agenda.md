# Format And Agenda

The SAAF hackathon is a **full day, in person, in two halves**. The
morning is **keynotes and presentations**; the afternoon — from 13:00
sharp — is **the hackathon itself**. The day ends at **17:00 sharp**
with drinks. Forty to a hundred participants.

This page describes the shape that has worked across SAAF NL sessions
and is recommended as the default starting point. Adapt — but adapt
deliberately.

---

## The Default Day

```
08:30  Doors open. Coffee, tea, light breakfast.
09:00  Welcome (15 min). Captain frames the day.
09:15  Keynote (30 min). External or internal — see speakers-and-keynotes.md.
09:45  Coffee break (15 min).
10:00  Presentation 1 (30 min).
10:30  Presentation 2 (30 min).
11:00  Coffee break (15 min).
11:15  Plan walkthrough + claim moment (45 min). Today's plans, tracks.
12:00  Lunch (60 min). Teams converge at their tables.
13:00  Hackathon starts. Build sprint 1 (90 min).
14:30  Coffee break + roaming check-ins (15 min).
14:45  Build sprint 2 (75 min).
16:00  Demo session (45 min).
16:45  Closing (15 min).
17:00  Drinks. Sharp end.
```

The morning is **roughly two hours of speaker content** wrapped in
breaks and ending with a plan walkthrough. The afternoon is **roughly
three hours of build time** ending with demos and a hard 17:00 close.

**The 17:00 close is sharp, not aspirational.** People have trains,
families, second jobs, evening lives. A SAAF day that drifts into the
evening trains people to stay home next time. The drinks immediately
after 17:00 give the social time without prolonging the formal
programme.

## The Two Halves

### Morning — keynotes and presentations (09:00–12:00)

The morning is for **shared context**. Auditors from different
organisations rarely sit in the same room; the morning is the moment
to put the same ideas, the same framing, the same shared examples in
front of everyone.

What goes in the morning:

- One **anchor keynote** (30 min) — a senior practitioner, an external
  voice with something sharp to say, or a vaktechniek lead from a
  member organisation. See
  [speakers-and-keynotes.md](speakers-and-keynotes.md) for how to
  pick and brief them.
- Two **presentations** (30 min each) — shorter talks from
  practitioners, researchers, or member-organisation auditors with a
  specific working example to share. Demos of agents that have
  reached the Check or Act stage are particularly valuable here.
- A **plan walkthrough** (45 min) — the captain or track leads walk
  the room through the day's plans, the tracks, and the claim
  moment. This is the bridge into the afternoon.

The morning is **not** a conference programme. Speakers are briefed
to teach something the room will use *that afternoon*. If a talk
doesn't connect to the build sprints that follow, it doesn't belong
in the morning.

### Afternoon — the hackathon (13:00–17:00)

The afternoon is for **building**. Teams form (most of this happened
informally at lunch), claim plans, and ship rough working artefacts
by 16:00. The shape is:

- Build sprint 1 (90 min).
- Coffee break (15 min).
- Build sprint 2 (75 min).
- Demos (45 min).
- Closing (15 min).
- Drinks at 17:00, sharp.

**Total build time: roughly 2 hours 45 minutes.** That is shorter
than a full-day hackathon and changes how plans are scoped — see
"Scoping for an afternoon hackathon" below.

## What Each Block Is For

### 08:30 — Doors open

**Coffee and tea on arrival are non-negotiable.** People come from
across a country, sometimes after a 06:00 train. The first thing they
see is hot drinks, food on a table, and someone who knows their name.

Light breakfast — fruit, pastries, yoghurt. Not a hot breakfast. The
day does not need full stomachs at 09:00.

### 09:00 — Welcome (15 min)

The captain frames the day in three things:

1. *Why we are here.* One sentence on the chapter's mission and the
   manifesto. Read one principle aloud.
2. *What today looks like.* The day's agenda on one slide. The split
   between morning (learn) and afternoon (build) is named explicitly.
3. *Practicalities.* Wifi password, bathrooms, retreat space, who to
   ask for what. End with: *"If you need anything, find [operator
   name] — they're wearing [colour]."*

If the captain runs over fifteen minutes, the day is already drifting.

### 09:15 — Keynote (30 min, hard cap)

One person, thirty minutes, hard cap. Full guidance in
[speakers-and-keynotes.md](speakers-and-keynotes.md). The keynote
sets the tone for the morning — and ideally seeds at least one idea
that teams will actually use after lunch.

### 09:45 — Coffee break (15 min)

Fresh coffee, water, light snacks. Fifteen minutes is short on purpose
— enough for a refill and a quick conversation, not enough for the
room to dissolve.

### 10:00 — Presentation 1 (30 min)

The second speaker slot. A practitioner case study, a tool demo
(non-product, see [speakers-and-keynotes.md](speakers-and-keynotes.md)),
or a methodology share from a member organisation. Hard time cap, same
discipline as the keynote.

### 10:30 — Presentation 2 (30 min)

The third speaker slot. Different angle from Presentation 1 to keep
the morning textured — for example, if Presentation 1 was a working
agent demo, Presentation 2 might be a regulatory or audit-quality
perspective on AI outputs.

### 11:00 — Coffee break (15 min)

A second short break. Energy is starting to dip after 90 minutes of
speaker content; the break is the reset before the plan walkthrough.

### 11:15 — Plan walkthrough + claim moment (45 min)

The captain or track leads walk through the day's plans. For each
plan:

- One sentence on what it is.
- Which track it lives in.
- Who claimed it (if anyone) before today.
- What kind of contributor it is looking for.

End with the **claim moment**: *"You have fifteen minutes during
lunch. Find a plan, find a team, sit at the table with the right
number on it. If you don't find a fit, find me at the buffet —
there's a plan looking for you."*

The 45-minute slot allows for a real walkthrough of 8–15 plans rather
than a rushed list-read.

### 12:00 — Lunch (60 min)

Plenty of food, plenty of variety, served all at once (no queues if
possible). Dietary needs met visibly — labels on every dish, not
"ask the server." See
[venue-logistics-and-comfort.md](venue-logistics-and-comfort.md).

Lunch is also **the team-formation block**. Tables are pre-marked by
track and plan number from 12:00 onward. Participants drift to the
table they want to work at and eat with their team. By 13:00 the
room is already in teams.

### 13:00 — Build sprint 1 (90 min)

**The hackathon starts.** Teams confirm their roster, share access,
pick the bit they will work on, and begin building. Captain and
facilitators circulate; do not interrupt unless asked.

This is the longest sprint of the day because teams need ramp-up
time. The captain walks every team in the first 30 minutes to
confirm everyone has a clear thing to ship.

### 14:30 — Coffee break + roaming check-ins (15 min)

Fresh coffee, water, snacks. A short break by design — fifteen
minutes is enough to refill and unblock conversations without
breaking momentum.

This is also the **roaming check-in**. The captain or a facilitator
visits every team to ask: *"What do you have? What's blocking you?
What can you actually demo at 16:00?"* Stuck teams get re-scoped
here, not in sprint 2.

### 14:45 — Build sprint 2 (75 min)

The final push. Captain and facilitators are visible but quiet. The
room should feel busy, not loud. A discreet snack table appears
around 15:30 — chocolate, fruit, biscuits — for the energy dip.

At 15:45, the captain announces: *"Fifteen minutes until demos. Get
to a demo-able state."* Teams that have been building toward "more"
need to compress; demo-readiness beats completeness.

### 16:00 — Demo session (45 min)

Five minutes per team, hard cap. A timer on screen. The captain runs
the timer and is willing to cut people off — gently — at the limit.

For each demo:

- 30 seconds: what we built and why.
- 3 minutes: live demo on synthetic data.
- 1 minute 30 seconds: questions.

With a 45-minute window, plan for **7–9 demos**. Chapters with more
than 9 teams either run two parallel demo rooms or rotate which
teams demo at which session.

The recap writer is taking detailed notes during this block.

### 16:45 — Closing (15 min)

The captain:

- Thanks the host organisation specifically and by name.
- Thanks the speakers, the volunteers, the operator.
- Names every team and every plan claimed today.
- Asks each team's PDCA owner for a one-line PDCA status update
  (Plan → Do → Check → Act).
- Announces the date and venue of the next session.
- Reads one sentence from the manifesto.

Closing should feel like a benediction, not a wrap-up meeting. Short
is good. **End at 17:00 on the dot.**

### 17:00 — Drinks

Drinks, light food, music optional. Captain and founding team
circulate. The same logic as the alignment-session drinks: this is
working time disguised as social time.

End drinks at a clear time — 19:00 or 19:30. People have trains.
Drinks that drift past 20:00 burn out the operators.

## Scoping For An Afternoon Hackathon

The afternoon build window is **roughly 2 hours 45 minutes**. That
is meaningfully tighter than a full-day hackathon, and it shapes
what teams can realistically ship. The captain's job is to make this
visible to the room *before* sprint 1 begins.

Practical scoping guidance for plan submitters and track leads:

- A plan that needed eight hours of build time in a previous format
  needs to be **carved down to a three-hour MVP** for this format.
- Teams should pick **one demo-able slice**, not the full plan.
- Prompts that have been pre-drafted, schemas already chosen, and
  synthetic data already loaded **count toward the afternoon's
  output** — they are not "cheating," they are part of why plans
  persist across sessions.
- A team that arrives knowing exactly what to build ships. A team
  that uses sprint 1 to decide what to build does not.

This is also why the plan walkthrough at 11:15 matters. By lunch,
every participant should know which plan they are joining and what
the first 30 minutes of sprint 1 will look like.

## When Forty Becomes A Hundred

The default agenda works up to roughly fifty people. Beyond that, you
need adjustments.

| Adjustment | Why |
|---|---|
| Two parallel demo rooms at 16:00 | A 100-person demo block at five minutes each is 8.5 hours. |
| Two facilitators per track minimum | One can't reach every team in a 15-min break. |
| Lunch served in two waves | Single-wave lunch creates a 25-minute queue at the buffet. |
| Multiple coffee stations | A single station bottlenecks during the 15-minute morning breaks. |
| A printed badge / table-sign system | Names and tracks must be visible without asking. |
| Pre-assigned tracks at registration | "Find a track" doesn't scale beyond fifty. |

Past 80, also consider:

- A separate "spectator track" for senior leaders who want to attend
  but not build. Park them in a single area with a facilitator who
  can rotate them through teams in the afternoon.
- A dedicated "first-timer" briefing at 08:45, fifteen minutes, to
  orient newcomers separately so they don't slow the welcome block.

## When You Have To Cut Time

Sometimes the venue closes earlier or the schedule starts late. The
17:00 close is sacred; everything else is negotiable. Priority for
cutting:

1. **Drop Presentation 2.** Move it to the next session.
2. **Compress the plan walkthrough to 30 minutes.** Skip the
   per-plan commentary and rely on the pre-circulated plan list.
3. **Cut the demo session to 30 minutes.** Fewer teams demo;
   non-demoing teams contribute their status in writing for the
   recap.
4. **Compress closing to 10 minutes.**
5. **Last resort: shorten Build sprint 1.** Build sprint 2 is
   sacred; do not shorten it.

Never shorten lunch below 50 minutes. The day collapses without it
— and the team-formation function of lunch is non-negotiable.

If the day starts late and you cannot recover, **end the morning
short and start the hackathon early** rather than pushing into the
evening. The 17:00 close holds.

## Hybrid And Remote Sessions

The default format assumes in-person. Hybrid is hard. Fully remote is
harder.

If you must run hybrid:

- The in-room work is the centre of gravity. Remote participants are
  participants, not observers.
- Each remote participant pairs with a "remote buddy" in the room,
  who keeps them connected to the team.
- Morning presentations and the 16:00 demo session are streamed;
  questions take 30 seconds longer because of audio lag.
- Drinks do not work remotely. Don't fake them.

If you must run fully remote:

- Compress the day. A three-to-four-hour remote hackathon (one
  presentation block + one build block + demos) is more productive
  than an eight-hour one.
- Use breakout rooms generously. The unstructured corridor of an
  in-person event has to be built explicitly online.
- Demo session is the main event. Plan accordingly.

The honest truth: SAAF works best in person. Remote is an emergency
fallback, not a default. The chapters that have tried fully-remote
sessions report lower energy, fewer plans claimed, and weaker recaps.
