# Glossary

Definitions of terms used throughout the playbook.

---

**Act.** The fourth stage of PDCA. Sharing back lessons learned
from a Check-stage pilot. A plan is *complete* when it has reached
Act.

**Agent.** A SAAF use-case type: an autonomous, multi-step
execution that has its own system prompt and calls one or more
tools to complete an audit task without continuous human steering.
See also *skill*, *workflow*.

**Alignment session.** A regional chapter's kickoff event. A
half-day meeting where the manifesto, the four pillars, and the
chapter's intent are placed in front of the room before the first
hackathon. Distinct from a hackathon.

**Ambassador.** Stage three of the advocacy funnel. A contributor
who represents the chapter externally — speaking at peer events,
recruiting new members, writing about the chapter's work.

**Captain.** The named person who holds a chapter. One per chapter.
Term typically 12–24 months. Defends the manifesto, runs sessions,
writes recaps, manages succession.

**Champion.** Stage four of the advocacy funnel. A member who
holds part of the chapter — track lead, facilitator, recap writer,
co-captain, or captain. The chapter's operating bench.

**Charter.** A one- to three-page document defining a chapter's
identity, scope, governance, IP terms, funding, and success
criteria. Reviewed at every captain rotation and annually.

**Check.** The third stage of PDCA. Validating a Do-stage artefact
inside an organisation, on real or anonymised data, behind the
firewall.

**Co-captain.** The captain's shadow and named successor. Runs
sessions when the captain is out. Non-negotiable role.

**Contribution flow.** The pull-request workflow that takes an
artefact from a chapter into the main SAAF Project repository.
See [../feeding-back-to-saaf/contribution-flow.md](../feeding-back-to-saaf/contribution-flow.md).

**Contributor.** Stage two of the advocacy funnel. A member whose
name is on a plan, a prompt, a tool, or a review.

**Do.** The second stage of PDCA. Building a working version of a
plan at a hackathon.

**Endorsing body.** A professional body (IIA, ISACA, NOREA, NBA,
ForHumanity, equivalent) that publicly endorses a regional chapter,
typically allowing members to count chapter participation toward
CPE / CPD hours. Endorsement is renewed annually.

**Facilitator.** A champion who runs tracks at sessions. Trained
on facilitation. Trusted to defend the manifesto live. See
[../running-hackathons/facilitation.md](../running-hackathons/facilitation.md).

**Federation.** The relationship between regional chapters and the
main SAAF Project. Federation = chapters are autonomous within
the manifesto; the national project maintains the manifesto and
the unqualified SAAF brand; artefacts flow back as a default.

**Floater.** A first-time participant who attends a session
without committing to a specific role. Joins a team in observer
mode for the first sprint, then settles into a role.

**Founding partners.** The 2–4 organisations that commit to the
launch of a regional chapter — sending builders to the first four
sessions, hosting at least one session in 18 months, contributing
plans.

**Four pillars.** The vocabulary of the SAAF community for
artefact organisation: Prompts, Tools, Regulatory, Outputs. Every
SAAF artefact lives in one of these four pillars.

**Hackathon.** A SAAF chapter's full-day, in-person session in two
halves: morning is keynotes and presentations (09:00–12:00),
afternoon is the build hackathon itself (13:00–17:00), drinks
immediately after at 17:00 sharp. See
[../running-hackathons/format-and-agenda.md](../running-hackathons/format-and-agenda.md).

**Internal chapter.** A SAAF programme inside a single
organisation. Chapter operates behind one organisation's firewall,
with senior-executive air cover, internal data access, and
internal-first sharing.

**Main SAAF Project.** The original SAAF community, founded
2026 in the Netherlands. Maintains the manifesto, the contribution
repository, and the federation. Operates under the SAAF Foundation.

**Manifesto.** The shared agreement across all SAAF chapters —
main, internal, and regional. The only document where all three
are identical. See [../MANIFESTO.md](../MANIFESTO.md).

**Operator-in-charge.** The person responsible for the logistics
of a session day. Owns wifi, AV, catering, the venue contact, the
equipment bag. Distinct from the captain.

**Participant.** Stage one of the advocacy funnel. A member who
attends sessions, reads recaps, and engages with the chapter without
yet contributing artefacts.

**PDCA.** Plan–Do–Check–Act. The cycle every SAAF plan moves
through, stretched across multiple sessions. See
[../foundations/what-saaf-is.md](../foundations/what-saaf-is.md).

**Plan.** The unit of work in a SAAF chapter. A one-page document
describing an agent, skill, or workflow that someone wants to
build. Plans persist across sessions.

**Plan portfolio.** The full set of a chapter's plans, by PDCA
stage. Reviewed quarterly.

**Professional Practices.** The audit-quality / methodology function
inside a firm or organisation. Professional Practices validates that
audit work is defensible against IIA / ISA / firm methodology. One of
the four SAAF roles. (In some firms this function is called
*vaktechniek*, *methodology*, or *audit quality*.)

**Recap.** The post-session document. Two to ten pages of
markdown. Lists what was built, who built it, lessons learned, and
next session details.

**Recap writer.** The named person who writes the recap. Doesn't
build on the day; documents the day.

**Regional chapter.** A SAAF chapter in a country, region, or
industry the main SAAF Project does not yet cover (e.g. SAAF
Belgium, SAAF Public Sector, SAAF DACH).

**Roles (in a SAAF session).** The four roles into which the room
is split: auditor, Professional Practices, analyst, engineer. Distinct from
*champion tier* (contributor, ambassador, champion) and from
*chapter operating roles* (captain, co-captain, facilitator,
operator).

**SAAF Foundation.** The legal entity that owns the SAAF name, the
"SAAF Project" name, the SAAF logo, and related marks. Only
*affiliated SAAF chapters* — internal, regional, or otherwise — may
use these marks. Affiliation is the formal recognition extended by
the Foundation to a chapter that operates within the manifesto.
Non-affiliated efforts may exist as communities, but cannot identify
themselves as SAAF.

**SAAF Portal.** The federation's participant-facing app, maintained
by the main SAAF Project. Members sign in with GitHub; the portal
provides profiles, the track-and-plan browser, the leaderboard,
the observability-check framework, badges, the participant
directory, and an agent library. The federation's operating surface
between sessions. Repo:
[github.com/SAAF-Project/saaf-portal](https://github.com/SAAF-Project/saaf-portal).
See [../measuring-value/saaf-portal.md](../measuring-value/saaf-portal.md).

**Skill.** A SAAF use-case type: a single-purpose reusable
capability, typically a prompt paired with a parsing function. See
also *agent*, *workflow*.

**Strapi sandbox.** An infrastructure pattern from the main SAAF
Project — a Docker-deployed Strapi instance that serves
synthetic audit data for testing. Often used as a synthetic-data
target by chapter agents.

**Synthetic data.** Test data that resembles real audit data
structurally without containing any real client information.
Required for all SAAF artefact development. See the manifesto.

**Track.** An organising vocabulary at a session — audit planning,
fieldwork, reporting, compliance, vendor risk, etc. Tracks let
participants find their team. See
[../running-hackathons/roles-and-tracks.md](../running-hackathons/roles-and-tracks.md).

**Track lead.** A champion who owns a track for a session. Walks
their tables, knows every team's plan, resolves stuck moments.

**Vendor-agnostic.** A core SAAF principle: artefacts must run on
at least two stacks; no exclusive vendor sponsorship; member
organisations choose their own deployment stacks. See the manifesto.

**Workflow.** A SAAF use-case type: an orchestrated sequence of
agents and/or skills with defined human checkpoints. See also
*agent*, *skill*.
