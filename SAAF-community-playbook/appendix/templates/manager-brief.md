# Manager Brief — Template

> A one-page note sent to a champion's manager *before* asking the
> champion to commit to a chapter role.
>
> The goal: secure the manager's understanding and consent up
> front, so the champion's contribution is not interpreted as
> distraction.

---

```
Subject: SAAF [Chapter Name] participation by [Champion's Name]

Hi [Manager's Name],

I'm [Captain's Name], captain of [Chapter Name], a [internal /
regional] chapter of the SAAF Project. SAAF — Shared Audit Agents
Framework — is a practitioner-led community where auditors,
Professional Practices leads, analysts, and engineers build AI audit agents
together.

I'd like to invite [Champion's Name] to take on a [specific role —
e.g. "track lead for the Compliance & Regulatory track at our
[date] session"] for the chapter. Before extending the formal
invitation, I wanted to brief you on what's involved and what
[Organisation] gets in return.

## What [Champion's Name] would be doing

- [Specific task 1]
- [Specific task 2]
- [Specific task 3]

## Time commitment

- [Estimated hours per quarter, broken down by activity]

The biggest commitment is the session day itself ([date],
[hours]).

## What [Organisation] gets

- Direct exposure to AI audit patterns from [N] other organisations.
- Early access to artefacts built in the chapter (prompts, control
  mappings, output schemas) that [Organisation]'s audit team can
  pilot internally.
- [Champion's Name]'s development through real, peer-reviewed
  contribution work.
- Visibility for [Organisation] in the SAAF community.

## What [Organisation] is asked for

- [Champion's Name]'s time at the rate above.
- Permission for [Champion's Name] to present internal work (with
  appropriate sanitisation) at chapter sessions.
- (Optional) Hosting one chapter session in the next 18 months —
  not required, available if [Organisation] would like.

## Manifesto and ground rules

The chapter operates under the SAAF Community Manifesto:
[link]. Highlights:

- Synthetic data only — no real audit evidence ever leaves
  [Organisation].
- Vendor-agnostic — the chapter doesn't promote any specific
  product.
- Co-creation, not competition — the chapter is the place where
  [Organisation] and other firms work on shared problems.

## Next step

If you're comfortable with [Champion's Name] taking this on, I'll
follow up with them directly. If you have questions or
reservations, I'd appreciate twenty minutes by phone or video to
walk through them.

Thanks for considering.

[Captain's Name]
[Chapter Name]
[Contact]
```

---

## Notes On Use

- Send this **before** asking the champion. Asking the manager
  *after* the champion has said yes can put the champion in a
  difficult position.

- Keep it **one page**. Managers triage email by length. A two-page
  brief gets skimmed; a one-page brief gets read.

- Be **specific** about the role. "Will be a champion" is too
  vague to evaluate. "Track lead for the Compliance & Regulatory
  track at our 14 March session, including pre-session plan
  reviews and 1 hour of facilitation on the day" is concrete.

- Be **honest** about the time. Underestimating to make it sound
  easy backfires when the champion's actual hours are double the
  estimate.

- Offer **a phone call**. Most managers won't take it. Some will,
  and it's the best 20-minute conversation you'll have all
  quarter.

- **Cc the champion** if the manager has agreed and you want to
  proceed. If the manager has reservations, do not cc the
  champion until those are resolved.
