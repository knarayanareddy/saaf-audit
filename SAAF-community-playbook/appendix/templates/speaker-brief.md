# Speaker Brief — Template

> The brief sent to a keynote speaker two weeks before a session.
> One page.

---

```
Subject: SAAF [Chapter Name] keynote brief — [Date]

Hi [Speaker's Name],

Thanks again for agreeing to keynote at [Chapter Name] on [Date].
This is the brief — please read it before the day.

## The chapter

[Chapter Name] is a [internal / regional] chapter of the SAAF
Project. SAAF stands for Shared Audit Agents Framework. The
chapter brings together auditors, Professional Practices leads, analysts, and
engineers from [N] organisations to build AI audit agents in the
open. Our manifesto is at [link].

## The audience

- ~[N] people, in person.
- Mix of roles: ~40% auditors / Professional Practices, ~40% engineers /
  analysts, ~20% senior leaders observing.
- Mix of seniority: from juniors to partners. Treat the room as
  technically literate but not uniformly engineering-trained.
- Mix of sectors: [list the dominant sectors represented].

What the audience cares about:

- Working AI audit patterns they can take home and try.
- Honest accounts of what's failed.
- Concepts at the level of detail needed to build, not at the
  level of "what is AI."

## Your slot

- **Duration:** 30 minutes. Hard cap.
- **Time:** 09:15 to 09:45 (keynote slot — adjust if you have been
  invited for one of the Presentation slots at 10:00 or 10:30).
- **Format:** Stand-up talk with slides if you want; live demo
  encouraged.
- **Q&A:** None in the slot itself. The morning programme runs back
  to back into a coffee break. You're welcome to take questions
  during the coffee break that follows.

## What a great talk looks like

- One sharp idea, made vivid.
- A live demo or a screenshot walkthrough — something concrete the
  audience can see, not just hear.
- An honest moment about what didn't work or what surprised you.
- Specifics over generalities throughout.

## What doesn't work

- A survey of the AI landscape.
- A history of your organisation's AI journey.
- Anything that reduces to "and our product / consultancy can
  help."
- Running over the time cap. The captain will signal at 5 minutes
  remaining and gently end the slot at the limit.

## Logistics

- **Arrive:** by 08:45 for AV check.
- **Address:** [Venue], [Address]
- **Contact on the day:** [Captain or Operator name and phone]
- **AV:** HDMI / USB-C available. Bring your own dongle as a
  backup. Slides on your own laptop is preferred.
- **Honorarium:** [Amount and payment method].

## What to expect

- A friendly room. Audit is a profession that gives speakers a
  hearing.
- Direct questions. Audit professionals fact-check live.
- Demos that don't quite work are forgiven; demos that pretend to
  work and don't are punished.

Thanks again. See you on [Date].

[Captain's Name]
[Chapter Name]
[Contact]
```

---

## Notes On Use

- Send **two weeks before** the session. Earlier and the speaker
  forgets; later and they don't have time to adjust.

- The brief is a **filter**. A speaker who reads it and pushes back
  on the time limit, the format, or the "no product mentions" rule
  is signalling something. Sometimes the right response is to
  re-confirm the rules; sometimes it's to invite a different
  speaker.

- Include the **honorarium** explicitly. Awkwardness about money
  ahead of time saves awkwardness about money on the day.

- For internal-chapter speakers (executive sponsor, internal senior
  auditor), most of the brief still applies but the honorarium line
  drops. The role-clarification lines become more important — an
  executive sponsor speaking might want to deliver an hour-long
  state-of-the-portfolio update; the brief redirects them to a
  20-minute "frame the day" slot folded into the welcome block.

- If the speaker is a **vendor's research team**, add an extra
  line: *"This slot is not a product slot. The talk should
  survive the removal of all product mentions. We will redirect
  product-shaped content gently if needed."*
