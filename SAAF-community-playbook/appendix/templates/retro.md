# After-Session Retrospective — Template

> The retrospective the captain and founding team run within seven
> days of every session. Distinct from the participant survey
> (which collects from attendees) and the recap (which is
> public-facing).
>
> The retro is internal, candid, and unpolished. It's where the
> chapter improves session-over-session.

---

## When To Run It

- **Within seven days** of the session, while memory is fresh.
- **In person or by video.** Not over email — the conversation
  matters.
- **45 to 60 minutes**, no longer.
- **Attendees:** captain, co-captain, operator-in-charge, recap
  writer, track leads. Keep it small.

## Structure

```markdown
# [Chapter Name] — Session [N] Retro
## [Date held]

## Attendees
- [Name]
- [Name]

## What worked

[2-5 specific things that worked. Each one a sentence or two.
"What worked" is *specific*, not "good energy."]

## What didn't work

[2-5 specific things that didn't work. Honest, not blaming. "The
keynote ran 12 minutes over and sprint 1 lost 12 minutes" is more
useful than "timing was tight."]

## What surprised us

[Things that happened that nobody expected. Sometimes positive
(an unexpected partnership emerging), sometimes negative (a vendor
behaving unexpectedly). Surprises are often where the most
durable lessons live.]

## Specific incidents to capture

[Anything that happened that's worth remembering specifically.
A team that had to re-form mid-day. A speaker who excelled or
fell short. A near-miss with a vendor incident. Capture the
detail; the lesson can be drawn later.]

## Lessons to file upstream

[1-3 lessons from this session that should be written up in the
lessons-learned format and contributed back to the main SAAF
Project. See feeding-back-to-saaf/lessons-learned.md for the
format.]

## Decisions for next session

[Concrete changes for the next session, with owners.]

| Change | Owner | Deadline |
|---|---|---|
| [Change 1] | [Name] | [Date] |
| [Change 2] | [Name] | [Date] |

## Things to keep doing the same

[The unsexy but important section. List the things that worked
this time and shouldn't be improved.]
```

---

## How To Run The Conversation

The captain or co-captain facilitates. Keep it light, structured,
and time-bounded.

A simple agenda:

| Time | What | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 0–5 min | Settle, agree the shape | The captain restates the four sections. |
| 5–15 min | What worked | Round-robin, one item per person, one minute each. |
| 15–30 min | What didn't work | Round-robin, with discussion as needed. |
| 30–40 min | Lessons to file upstream | Pick the 1–3 worth writing up. |
| 40–55 min | Decisions for next session | Each item has an owner before it's added to the list. |
| 55–60 min | Anything else | Catch-all. |

### Norms during the retro

- **Specific over general.** "The catering line was 25 minutes" is
  better than "lunch was slow."
- **Behaviour over personality.** "The keynote went over by 12
  minutes" is better than "[Speaker] is undisciplined."
- **No defending.** When someone names something that didn't work,
  the response is "noted" or "tell me more," not "well actually..."
- **Action items have owners.** "We should ___" is incomplete.
  "[Name] will ___ by [date]" is complete.

### What the captain captures

The captain (or a designated note-taker) writes the retro document
during the conversation and circulates it within 24 hours. Owners
of action items confirm acceptance by reply.

---

## Filing The Retro

Retros are **internal to the chapter's operating team**. Do not
publish them. Do not share them with the executive sponsor or the
endorsing body.

Why: the value of the retro is its candor. Candor erodes if the
retro becomes a public document. A public version of the retro
already exists — it is the recap.

The lessons that should travel further get written up in the
lessons-learned format and contributed to the main SAAF
Project. The retro itself is private to the operating team.

## A Pattern To Watch For

After three or four sessions, scan back through the retros. If the
same "what didn't work" item appears in multiple retros, the
chapter has a chronic issue, not a one-off.

Common chronic issues:

- The captain consistently underestimates set-up time on session
  morning.
- Plan submissions consistently arrive in the final 48 hours
  instead of the 2-week deadline.
- The same one or two participants are consistently disengaged.
- The same one or two organisations consistently send people who
  attend but don't contribute.

Chronic issues need a different response than one-offs — usually a
structural change (a new pre-session deadline, a different
recruitment approach, a conversation with a partner organisation).
The retro is where chronic issues become visible; the response is
captain-led.
