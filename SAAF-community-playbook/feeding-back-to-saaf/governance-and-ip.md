# Governance And IP

The IP terms, attribution rules, and dispute-resolution paths between
chapters, the main SAAF Project, and the **SAAF Foundation**.

This page is deliberately conservative. It does not promise terms
that the Foundation and the main project organisers haven't actually
committed to; it points at the live terms in the SAAF-Project
repository where those exist, and gives chapter founders the questions
to ask.

---

## The SAAF Foundation Owns The Marks

The **SAAF name**, the name **"SAAF Project"**, the **SAAF logo**, and
related marks are owned by the **SAAF Foundation**. They are not in
the public domain; they are not free to use; they are not owned by
the main SAAF Project, by any individual organiser, or by any
contributor.

**Only affiliated SAAF chapters may use the marks.** Affiliation is
the formal recognition extended by the Foundation to a chapter that:

- Adopts the [manifesto](../MANIFESTO.md) verbatim.
- Operates under the four pillars (Prompts, Tools, Regulatory,
  Outputs).
- Qualifies its name appropriately (e.g. "SAAF Belgium," not "SAAF").
- Connects to the main SAAF Project's repository and platforms (see
  [../engagement-and-growth/communication-channels.md](../engagement-and-growth/communication-channels.md)).
- Acts in good faith within the federation.

A community of auditors building AI audit agents together is welcome
to exist *without* affiliation — but it cannot identify itself as
SAAF, use the SAAF logo, or imply SAAF Foundation endorsement. It can
be a great community; it just needs a different name.

### What affiliation looks like in practice

Affiliation is a conversation, not an application form. A founding
team that wants to start an affiliated chapter should:

1. **Reach out to the main SAAF Project organisers** (see
   [../internal-chapter/README.md#get-in-touch-with-the-main-saaf-project-first](../internal-chapter/README.md#get-in-touch-with-the-main-saaf-project-first)).
2. Walk through the chapter's intent, scope, founding team, and
   manifesto adoption.
3. Agree the name (qualified appropriately) and the relationship to
   the federation.
4. Receive Foundation acknowledgement of affiliation, in writing.

Once affiliated, the chapter uses the SAAF marks under the terms set
out below and in the Foundation's brand guidelines (maintained
separately by the Foundation; ask the main project organisers for
the current version).

### What affiliation does not include

- **Approval rights over chapter content.** The Foundation does not
  pre-approve plans, prompts, sessions, recaps, or hackathon
  agendas. Chapters operate autonomously within the manifesto.
- **Funding from the Foundation.** Affiliation is recognition, not a
  grant.
- **A right to bind the Foundation.** Chapters cannot sign contracts,
  partnerships, or sponsorship agreements on behalf of the
  Foundation or the main SAAF Project.

### Loss of affiliation

The Foundation reserves the right to withdraw affiliation if a
chapter operates outside the manifesto in serious or persistent
ways (vendor capture, real-data sharing, brand misuse,
harassment-grade conduct that the captain refuses to address). This
is rare and is preceded by direct conversation; it has not been
exercised lightly in the federation's history. A chapter that loses
affiliation retains its prior contributions in the main repository
(with attribution preserved) but ceases to use the SAAF marks going
forward.

## Where The Live Terms Live

The main SAAF Project's repository contains its own license
file, contribution guidelines, and any IP terms applicable to the
artefacts in the four pillars. The **SAAF Foundation** maintains the
brand guidelines and the affiliation terms separately. **All of those
documents are authoritative.**

A chapter charter that promises terms different from what the main
project repository or the Foundation specifies will create
confusion. Chapters should:

- Reference, not duplicate, the main project's license terms.
- Adopt the main project's contribution guidelines as the chapter's.
- Defer to the Foundation's brand guidelines for any use of the SAAF
  marks.
- Specify chapter-only matters (chapter governance, chapter
  endorsements) without trying to redefine federation-wide IP.

If a chapter has a strong reason to deviate from the main project's
terms (e.g. a regulator-required clause, or a region-specific IP
regime), the right path is a conversation with the main project
organisers (and, for brand matters, the Foundation), not a
unilateral charter clause.

## Attribution

Attribution is the strictest part of SAAF's governance. The rules:

- **Every artefact carries the contributor's name** (real or
  pseudonym), the chapter, and the session.
- **Attribution is preserved across the artefact's lifetime.**
  Forking, adapting, and extending an artefact preserves the
  original attribution; the new contributor adds their name without
  removing the original.
- **Attribution is not transferable.** A vendor that uses a
  community-contributed artefact internally cannot rebrand it as
  their own. If they reference it externally, they reference the
  contributor and the chapter.
- **Pseudonyms are valid attribution.** A pseudonym used
  consistently across artefacts is the attribution, and it is
  protected on the same terms as a real name.

Attribution disputes are rare in well-functioning chapters. When
they arise, the captain mediates within the chapter; if that fails,
the national project organisers mediate.

## What Chapters Can And Cannot License Out

A chapter does not own the artefacts its members contribute. The
artefacts are owned by their contributors and licensed under the
main SAAF Project's terms.

Practical implications:

- **A chapter cannot grant exclusive rights to a vendor.** Even if
  the vendor sponsored the chapter heavily, the artefacts were
  licensed to the community, not to the chapter to re-license.
- **A chapter cannot retroactively privatise a contributed
  artefact.** Once an artefact is in the community repository, it
  stays there under its existing license.
- **A chapter can decline to contribute new artefacts.** If a
  chapter decides a particular artefact should remain internal, it
  doesn't have to be contributed. But it cannot be both contributed
  and reserved.

These rules exist to keep the community's repository trustworthy.
Members contribute because they trust the artefacts won't be
silently captured later.

## Vendor Use Of Community Artefacts

Vendors are explicitly welcome to use community-contributed
artefacts in their products, services, and customer engagements,
under the national project's license terms. This includes:

- Building products that incorporate community prompts, with
  attribution.
- Using community control mappings in commercial audit-tooling
  products, with attribution.
- Bundling community schemas as their default output format, with
  attribution.

Vendors are not permitted to:

- Claim authorship of community artefacts.
- Remove attribution.
- License community artefacts to others on terms different from the
  community license.
- Imply endorsement by the chapter or by the national project unless
  explicitly granted.

When vendors have questions, the answer is usually in the
repository's license file. When the answer is not clear, the
default answer is "the same terms as anyone else in the community."

## Real Audit Data

Real audit data does not flow through the chapter or the federation
under any circumstances. This is non-negotiable.

If a chapter, sponsor, or vendor requests that real data be shared
"just within the community," the answer is no. The response is
clear:

> The chapter only works with synthetic or anonymised data. Real
> audit evidence stays inside the firewall it came from. This is
> in the manifesto, the chapter charter, and the national project's
> contribution guidelines. There is no version of this where we make
> an exception.

A request for real data is sometimes the first signal of a more
serious problem (a chapter being captured, a vendor pressuring for
training data, a sponsor wanting case studies they shouldn't have).
The captain treats the request as a signal worth investigating.

## Confidentiality And NDAs

The chapter does not operate under NDAs. Members participate as
themselves; they do not bring confidential information from their
employers to the chapter.

Specifically:

- **A chapter does not sign a multi-party NDA.** Any cross-firewall
  exchange happens at the level of synthesised patterns, not data.
- **A vendor sponsor cannot require attendees to sign an NDA before
  attending a session.** If a vendor's sponsorship is contingent on
  this, decline the sponsorship.
- **An individual member organisation can ask its own employees to
  follow internal confidentiality rules.** That is between the
  organisation and its employees. The chapter does not enforce or
  refer to those rules.

The result: chapter conversations stay at the level of *categories
of audit problems*, not *specific instances*. A discussion of vendor
risk practices is fine; a discussion of any specific vendor's
specific contract is not.

## Disputes

Disputes between chapter members, between chapters, or between a
chapter and the national project are rare. When they happen:

### Within a chapter

The captain mediates. If the dispute is between the captain and
another member, the co-captain mediates. If both are involved, the
endorsing body's senior contact mediates. If the dispute remains
unresolved, the chapter has a serious problem and the national
project organisers are notified — but they do not arbitrate; they
support whatever resolution the chapter decides.

### Between chapters

Captain-to-captain conversation, in private, with the national
project organisers informed. If the conversation does not resolve
the dispute, the national project organisers facilitate a three-way
discussion. There is no formal arbitration; the federation runs on
goodwill.

### Between a chapter and the national project

The chapter captain and the national project organisers have a
direct conversation. If the dispute is about the manifesto or the
license, the conversation is taken to the next annual organiser
sync for broader discussion.

If the dispute is irresolvable, the chapter's options are: amend
its own behaviour, propose a manifesto amendment, or fork (see
[cross-chapter-sync.md](cross-chapter-sync.md#when-a-chapter-forks)).

### Personal conduct

If a member behaves in a way that violates basic professional
standards — harassment, discrimination, unwanted contact — the
captain acts immediately, regardless of how senior or how
historically valuable the person is. The member is removed from the
session; if the conduct is serious, from the chapter.

The chapter does not need a formal code of conduct document to act
on this. The captain's authority to act is implicit in the role.

A chapter can, and should, write a brief code of conduct anyway —
not as a legal document but as a public statement of expectations.
A few sentences are enough. Many established practitioner communities
publish short, well-tested codes of conduct that can be adapted.

## What This Page Doesn't Try To Do

- Provide a complete legal framework. (See the national project's
  license.)
- Anticipate every IP edge case. (Bring it to a captain conversation.)
- Replace legal advice. (When in doubt, ask a lawyer.)
- Specify terms different from the national project's. (Don't.)

The page is meant to be enough that a chapter can operate, recognise
issues early, and route them to the right place. Everything beyond
that is handled case by case.
