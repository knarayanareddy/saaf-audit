# Cross-Chapter Sync

How chapters talk to each other, and to the main SAAF Project,
without overhead.

The federation model is intentionally flat. There is no SAAF central
office. There is no chapter approval process. There is no
mandatory reporting line. What there is: a small number of voluntary
moments where captains and organisers actually talk to each other.

---

## The Annual Organiser Sync

Once a year, the main SAAF Project organisers convene the
captains of all known chapters — internal and regional — for a
half-day sync.

### Format

- Half day, often virtual to accommodate geography.
- Attended by: national project organisers, every chapter's captain
  (or co-captain if captain unavailable), one founding-team member
  per chapter optional.
- Three blocks: each chapter shares (briefly), shared challenges
  discussion, manifesto review.

### What each chapter shares

Five minutes per chapter:

- One number: how the chapter has changed in the past year (members,
  plans, completions).
- One thing that worked.
- One thing that didn't work.
- One ask of the federation.

Five minutes is a hard cap. Larger chapters with more to share write
a longer note in advance and circulate it; the live time is for
*new* information.

### What gets discussed in the shared challenges block

The captains pool problems they're facing. Common themes:

- Sponsor turnover and rebuilding executive air cover.
- Vendor pressure and how to handle aggressive sponsors.
- Plan portfolios that have grown too large.
- Captain succession.
- Specific technical challenges (the same MCP, the same RAG
  problem) that multiple chapters are hitting.

The output is not a decision; it is a shared awareness. Sometimes
two captains find they should pair on a specific issue. Sometimes a
challenge surfaces that the national project organisers can address.

### The manifesto review block

Once a year, the manifesto is read aloud and reviewed by the
captains. The question: *"Is anything in this manifesto wrong?"*

Most years, the answer is no. Once every few years, an amendment
emerges from this conversation. Amendments are proposed in writing,
discussed in the open, and adopted by consensus among active
captains. There is no formal voting threshold; if any captain
strongly objects, the amendment is reworked or dropped.

## The Quarterly Captain Call

Optional, ad-hoc, by invitation.

The national project organisers host a sixty-minute call once a
quarter. Captains attend if they have something to discuss or if
they want to listen in. Most quarters, half the chapters attend.
That's fine — attendance is voluntary.

### What's on the agenda

- Anything any captain wants to put on it.
- Quick updates from the national project organisers on roadmap,
  upcoming sessions, and infrastructure changes.
- Cross-chapter pairing on shared challenges.

### What's not on the agenda

- Reporting against metrics (each chapter does that internally, not
  upward).
- Approval of chapter-level decisions.
- Vendor management (each chapter handles its own).

The call is for **conversations that benefit from being live**. If
the topic is fine in writing, it goes in writing. The call is short
and cheap because most things don't need it.

## Captain-To-Captain Pairing

The most underused mechanism in the federation is direct captain-
to-captain conversation. The annual sync and quarterly calls are
useful, but the highest-leverage cross-chapter exchange is two
captains on a video call talking about a specific problem.

Examples that have worked elsewhere:

- A new captain shadows a more experienced captain's session day,
  in person, and writes a one-page note about what they observed.
- Two captains facing the same vendor pressure compare notes and
  draft a joint response.
- A captain considering captain rotation asks a captain who has
  already rotated about how it went.

There is no formal mechanism for this. The national project
organisers maintain a list of captains and their contacts; any
captain can ask any other captain for a conversation. The
expectation is that captains say yes when reasonably possible.

## Chapter-To-National-Project Communication

Outside the annual sync and quarterly calls, the channels are:

- **The national project's GitHub repository.** PRs, issues,
  discussions. Most contribution flow happens here.
- **Email to the national project organisers.** For sensitive or
  off-public-record matters.
- **The chapter's recap distribution.** The national project
  organisers are on the recap distribution of every chapter that
  invites them.

What does not exist:

- A formal escalation path inside the federation.
- A dispute-resolution committee.
- A "national board" with authority over chapters.

The federation is small and runs on relationships. When relationships
break — which is rare but possible — the parties involved escalate
to whichever endorsing body is most relevant, and the federation
absorbs the change.

## When A Chapter Forks

Sometimes a chapter decides to leave the federation. This is rare
and usually accompanies a specific disagreement (manifesto
amendment direction, IP terms, vendor relationships).

The process:

1. The chapter's captain notifies the main project organisers and
   the **SAAF Foundation** in writing.
2. **The chapter's SAAF Foundation affiliation ends.** The chapter
   ceases to use the SAAF name, the "SAAF Project" name, and the
   SAAF logo. "SAAF Belgium" becomes something else entirely —
   not "SAAF Belgium (independent)," not "ex-SAAF Belgium," just a
   new name.
3. The contributed artefacts remain in the main project's
   repository, with attribution preserved. The chapter's
   contributions to date are not retracted.
4. The chapter's onward work is independent and unaffiliated.

Forking is allowed and not stigmatised. Communities outgrow each
other; the manifesto is meant to be opt-in, not a cage. The mark
rules are not a punishment for forking; they are how affiliation
works — affiliation grants the marks, ending affiliation ends the
mark use.

## When Two Chapters Merge

Also rare. Sometimes two chapters in adjacent geographies (e.g.
SAAF DACH and SAAF Switzerland) decide they would be stronger as
one.

The process:

1. The captains of both chapters agree.
2. A merger plan is written, including: new name, new captain (or
   co-captain pair), new founding-partner roster, transition timing.
3. The national project organisers are notified.
4. The merged chapter's first session re-introduces the merged
   identity and re-affirms the manifesto.

Mergers are healthy when they reduce duplication and grow the room.
They are unhealthy when they paper over a struggling chapter by
attaching it to a stronger one. Merge for strength, not for
rescue.

## Cross-chapter Visiting

The most fun moment in the federation is when a member of one
chapter attends another chapter's session. It is also one of the
most valuable moments — a captain from another chapter sees the
format with fresh eyes and contributes observations the home
captain has stopped seeing.

The mechanics:

- A captain who wants to visit another chapter's session emails
  that chapter's captain six weeks ahead.
- The visiting captain attends as a participant, not as a speaker —
  they join a team, claim a plan, build alongside the locals.
- The visiting captain may give a short, optional 5-minute "what
  we're seeing in our chapter" share at the closing block, if the
  hosting captain invites it.
- After the session, the visiting captain writes a short note to
  their own chapter, summarising what they observed and what they
  brought home.

This is one of the strongest forms of cross-chapter sync available.
It compounds: chapters that exchange visits regularly converge on
the best practices faster than chapters that only read each other's
recaps.

## A Note On Time Investment

The federation's overhead, summed up:

- Annual sync: half a day per captain.
- Quarterly calls: 1 hour × 4 = 4 hours per captain (optional).
- Contribution flow: a few PRs per quarter.
- Cross-chapter visits: zero to two per year per captain (optional).

Total: roughly 1 to 2 days per year per captain.

That is the right order of magnitude. The federation is designed
not to require more. A federation that demands ten days of captain
time per year would gradually deter chapters from joining at all.
