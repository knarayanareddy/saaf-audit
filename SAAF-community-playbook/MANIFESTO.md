# The SAAF Community Manifesto

> **Shared Audit Agents Framework**
> Think Big · Start Small · Move Fast
> Learning by building.

---

## The Vision

Our vision is to contribute to audit functions evolving into a
predictive, high-quality, and fully inclusive assurance partner through
AI automation and radical knowledge sharing. This involves establishing
a shared framework through alignment sessions and 10 hackathons,
eliminating the need for individual research. Every person joining the
SAAF Project will contribute in their own way, choosing between tools,
prompts, compliance, or infrastructure-related tasks. We will work hard
over the hackathons to advance our knowledge and collectively build
automations, leveraging each person's unique expertise in areas like
prompting, tools, policies, and workflows.

---

## The Mission

Our mission is to strengthen Internal Audit with responsible, AI-driven
automation that shortens lead time, increases audit depth, and achieves
demonstrable, lasting quality improvement in our audits. We accomplish
this by fostering collaboration and knowledge-sharing around shared,
non-competitive AI agents, skills, workflows, and automations. We strive
to develop high-quality AI Agents and workflows that can be utilised in
audits or serve as a foundational knowledge base for new, similar
automations, which is achieved by organising individuals based on skill
level for training and agent development, ensuring mutual support within
the teams, and actively sharing collective "Do's, Don'ts, and Lessons
Learned" for all AI Agents, Skills, and Automations. The ultimate goal
is to maintain an open, reusable knowledge base that every auditor,
regardless of knowledge or experience level, can immediately apply.

## What We Believe

**Audit is a profession, not a vendor's roadmap.** AI tools come and go.
Audit standards, professional judgement, and the trust placed in our work
do not. We build for the profession first; the tooling follows.

**Co-creation beats competition.** None of us has the budget, the people,
or the data to solve audit's AI questions alone. Together, the same hour of
thinking compounds across dozens of teams.

**Domain expertise is the rarest input.** Code is cheap. Models are cheap.
A senior auditor who has run two hundred control tests is not. The community
exists to put domain expertise in front of the work, not behind it.

**Learning by building.** We do not run a working group. We do not write
position papers. We meet, we ship, we share what worked and what didn't,
and we come back next session to do it better.

**Vendor-agnostic by default.** We work with whichever model, framework,
or stack does the job. Lock-in is a risk, not a strategy. Our shared
artefacts must run wherever a member organisation needs them to run.

**Open over proprietary, where possible.** Findings, prompts, control
mappings, and templates default to open. Organisations keep the data,
the deployments, and the client confidentiality — they do not need to
keep the patterns.

**Synthetic data only.** Real audit evidence stays inside the firewall it
came from. Everything we share, demo, or commit uses synthetic or
anonymised data. No exceptions.

**Plan · Do · Check · Act.** Every plan moves through PDCA across
sessions: plan it at one hackathon, build it at the next, validate it
quietly inside an organisation, and bring back the lessons learned.

## What We Commit To

As a SAAF community member — main, internal, or regional — we commit to:

**1. Shipping over presenting.** A rough working agent at the end of the
day beats a polished slide deck about a future agent. Demos, not decks.

**2. Sharing back.** Anything built using community time, community data,
or community infrastructure flows back to the community as a prompt,
template, mapping, or lesson. The default is share; the exception is
withhold, and the exception needs a reason.

**3. Hosting in turn.** No one organisation owns the venue, the agenda,
or the brand. Member organisations host hackathons in rotation. The
community travels.

**4. Inviting the next level of expertise.** Every session has space for
someone who has never been before, and space for someone whose bar is
higher than ours. We grow at both ends.

**5. Naming attribution.** Plans, prompts, and tools carry the name of
the person who built them — pseudonyms permitted — so contribution is
visible and credit travels with the artefact.

**6. Defending the firewall.** No real client data, no production
credentials, no live PII in shared workspaces. If you wouldn't put it on
a public Confluence page at your own organisation, it does not belong in
the SAAF repo.

**7. Explaining the output.** Every agent we ship answers the question
"how did you arrive at this?" Black-box findings are not findings.
Auditability is non-negotiable; the audit profession built the word.

**8. Translating value upward.** Internal champions and regional leads
commit to making the value of community work legible to their executives
in the executives' own language — risk reduced, hours returned, decisions
clarified — not in vanity metrics.

**9. Calling things finished.** When a plan reaches Act, we mark it done,
write the lesson, and move on. Communities decay when half-built projects
accumulate.

**10. Protecting the spirit.** When sponsorship pressure, vendor capture,
or scope creep threatens the manifesto, we name it out loud and push back.
The manifesto exists so that no single voice — not a sponsor, not an
organiser, not a host — can quietly redefine what SAAF is.

---

## How To Use This Manifesto

Read it at the start of every alignment session. Print it on the back of
the table signs. Quote it when someone asks why we are working this way.
Update it when the community decides — by consensus of active contributors,
not by decree — that something here no longer holds.

If a section of this manifesto is in conflict with how we are actually
operating, the manifesto is wrong, or we are. Either way, we fix it.
