# SAAF Community Playbook

> A field guide for starting a SAAF-style programme — inside your own
> organisation, or as a regional chapter — and bringing the learnings back
> to the main SAAF Project.

This is the playbook for **everyone after the first wave**.

The main SAAF Project (NL) was founded in 2026 by Eduward van de Kamp and 
Mathijs Scholten, auditors who decided to build AI audit agents together, 
across organisations, in the open. It worked. People in other countries, 
and people inside individual organisations, started asking the same question:

> *"How do we run SAAF hackathons here — and how do our learnings make
> it back to the wider SAAF community?"*

This playbook answers that question. It is built around three things:

1. **Running SAAF hackathons inside your own organisation** — the
   internal-chapter path.
2. **Running SAAF hackathons regionally** — across organisations in a
   new country or sector — the regional-chapter path.
3. **Feeding the learnings, artefacts, and outcomes back to the
   main SAAF Project** — the same path for both.

## Two Playbooks, One Manifesto

The community has two distinct shapes that share the same DNA:

**Internal SAAF** lives inside a single organisation — a bank, a Big Four
firm, a regulator, an industrial group. Its job is to turn that
organisation's auditors into builders, with executive air cover, a budget,
and access to internal systems and data. Champions emerge inside teams.
Output flows to the organisation first, and back to the community second.

**Regional SAAF** is a cross-organisation chapter in a geography or
industry the main SAAF Project does not yet cover — SAAF Belgium, SAAF
DACH, SAAF Financial Services, SAAF Public Sector. Its job is to bring
together auditors who would not otherwise share a room. Champions emerge
across organisations. 

Both run the same hackathon format. Both follow the same manifesto. Both
live and die by the same principles. They differ on governance, branding,
funding, IP, and how they relate to executives — which is why they have
separate playbook sections. Output flows back to the main SAAF Project as
prompts, mappings, and lessons learned.

If you are running both at once (an internal programme **and** a regional
chapter), read both sections. They compose; they do not collide.

## The Four SAAF Pillars

Every SAAF chapter — national, internal, or regional — organises shared
work around the same four pillars. They are explained in
[foundations/what-saaf-is.md](foundations/what-saaf-is.md), but as a
quick reference:

| Pillar | What it holds |
|---|---|
| **Prompts** | Domain-specific audit prompts and system instructions |
| **Tools** | Scripts, APIs, integrations, MCP servers |
| **Regulatory** | Control mappings, guardrails, AI usage policies |
| **Outputs** | Standardised report formats, finding schemas, templates |

If you are not building toward these four pillars, you are building
something other than SAAF. That is fine — but call it something else, so
the brand keeps its meaning.

## How This Playbook Was Built

The initial SAAF Community Playbook was created by **Imran Nashir** in
2026, drawing on the lived experience of the main SAAF Project — the
sessions, the plans, the recaps, the lessons learned along the way.
Where the playbook talks about champion programmes, advocacy funnels,
executive translation, or value measurement, it reflects established
patterns from community practice generally; the SAAF-specific shape
is layered on top.

SAAF-specific patterns — PDCA across sessions, the four pillars, the
auditor / Professional Practices / analyst / engineer role split —
come from the SAAF Project itself.

### Acknowledgement

The **[OWASP Security Champions Guide](https://securitychampions.owasp.org)**
served as an inspiration for this playbook — particularly its
manifesto-and-principles shape, its emphasis on how a champions
programme survives personnel changes, and its insistence that trust,
investment, and recognition are what keep champions in role over time.
SAAF is a different community, in a different profession, with
different artefacts; the debt of inspiration is still worth naming.

## Contributing Back

This playbook is itself a SAAF artefact — it follows the same rules as
prompts, tools, and templates. If you run a SAAF chapter and learn
something that contradicts or extends what is written here, open a pull
request. Pseudonyms permitted; attribution preserved.

The fastest way to make a future organiser's life easier is to write down
what you wish someone had told you on day one.

## A Note On Voice

The playbook tries to be useful before it is polished. Some sections are
prescriptive; others give options and ask you to choose. Where the
authors are confident, the language is confident. Where the community
hasn't yet learned the answer, the playbook says so and asks you to come
back when you have.

Read [MANIFESTO.md](MANIFESTO.md) before anything else.
