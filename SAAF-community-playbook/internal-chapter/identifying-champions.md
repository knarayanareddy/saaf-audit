# Identifying And Recruiting Internal Champions

You are not appointing champions. You are *recognising* them.

The single most important insight from prior community work is that
durable champions are not created top-down. They are people who already
behave the way you want a champion to behave; your job is to find them
and give them air cover.

The pattern observed across mature internal community programmes is
specific: champions are the people who **reference community context
when explaining their decisions**.

In a SAAF context, the equivalent signal is: they reference *building*
when they explain their decisions. They have a side project. They
already prompted ChatGPT to draft a finding, and they have opinions
about how it went.

---

## What An Internal Champion Looks Like

In the SAAF context, an internal champion is an employee who:

- Has audit, risk, or controls responsibility in their day job, *or* is
  a Professional Practices / methodology / audit-tooling person.
- Is recognised by peers as someone who "gets it" — either the audit
  side or the AI side, ideally both.
- Will spend genuine time (not stolen lunch breaks) on community work,
  because they believe it benefits their actual job.
- Has line-manager support (or at least line-manager indifference) to
  do so.
- Can speak in public about a piece of work without making it about
  themselves.

You are looking for **competence + reliability + generosity**, in that
order. Charisma is a bonus, not a requirement. The most effective
internal champions are often quiet seniors who simply ship.

---

## Where To Find Them

Champions are usually already producing the signal. You just have to
look in the right places.

**Internal Slack / Teams channels** with names like #ai-experiments,
#audit-tooling, #data-analytics-questions. Read the last three months.
The people who answer questions are champion candidates. The people who
ask thoughtful questions are champion candidates. The people who only
vent are not.

**The people running the awkward "AI lunch & learn"** that nobody asked
them to run. They are doing community work without permission. Give
them permission and a stage.

**The audit-quality / Professional Practices team.** They already think about
methodology, defensibility, and how to scale judgement. They are the
keepers of the standard your agents must meet, and they make excellent
champions — *especially* for the role of approving outputs.

**The audit data analytics group**, if you have one. They have already
solved the synthetic-data problem and the access-controls problem.
Recruit them; do not try to rebuild what they have built.

**Recent IT-audit hires from a Big Four firm.** They have seen the
firm-internal versions of this and can tell you what to copy and what
to skip.

**Anyone who has ever shipped a Power Automate flow, a Python script,
or a TeamMate macro that became "the way the team does it."** They are
already proving they can build artefacts that other people adopt.

## How To Recruit Without Burning Their Capital

A champion's credibility inside their team is the most valuable thing
they bring to the chapter. Burn it carelessly and you lose both the
champion and the goodwill.

**Talk to their manager first, not after.**
Send the manager a one-page note (template:
[appendix/templates/manager-brief.md](../appendix/templates/manager-brief.md))
explaining what the chapter is, what time commitment you are asking
for, and what the manager's team gets in return. Ask for a yes before
you ask the candidate.

**Give them a defined role from day one.**
"Will you be a champion" is too abstract. "Will you facilitate the
auditor track at our first session in [date]" is specific, scoped, and
easy to say yes to. Once they have done the specific thing, they
self-identify as part of the chapter.

**Make their first ask easy and visible.**
First asks should take less than four hours and produce something
visible inside the organisation. Reviewing two plan submissions.
Hosting a thirty-minute show-and-tell on something they already built.
Drafting a single prompt template from an existing audit work
programme. Not "build the agent."

**Acknowledge effort publicly, in their manager's hearing.**
A line in a quarterly all-hands. A name in the recap email cc'd to
their manager. A mention in the executive update. This is how
community work compounds inside an organisation.

**Never trade their time for free vendor labour.**
A champion who spends six months piloting a vendor's product on behalf
of the chapter is not a champion. They are unpaid sales engineering.
If you find yourself asking a champion to do that, stop.

## How Many Champions, And When To Stop Recruiting

For a first internal chapter:

| Stage | Champion count | What they do |
|---|---|---|
| Founding (months 0–3) | 3–5 | Co-design the chapter, run the first session |
| Early (months 3–9) | 6–10 | Each owns a track, a recap section, or a recurring artefact |
| Mature (months 9+) | 10–25 | Specialise — facilitators, plan reviewers, content reviewers, recruiters |

Stop recruiting when you cannot meaningfully use the next champion's
time. Champions without real work to do disengage quickly and the
"former champion" effect is costly.

If you are recruiting your eleventh champion and the first three are
disengaged, you have a retention problem, not a recruitment problem.
Read [engagement-and-growth/rebuilding-participation.md](../engagement-and-growth/rebuilding-participation.md).

## Champion Tiers Inside An Organisation

It helps to be explicit about what tier of champion someone is, so
expectations match.

**Contributor.** Has shown up, written something, reviewed something.
No commitment beyond the next session.

**Track lead.** Owns a recurring track or topic (e.g. controls
mapping, evidence extraction, regulatory monitoring). Reviews plans in
that track before sessions.

**Facilitator.** Runs sessions or parts of sessions. Trained on the
[running-hackathons/facilitation.md](../running-hackathons/facilitation.md)
playbook. Trusted to defend the manifesto live.

**Captain (and co-captain).** Holds the chapter. One named per
chapter, with a named understudy. Rotates every 12–24 months.

The tiers are not promotions. People can be in any tier indefinitely.
The point is to make the *shape* of the contribution legible to the
champion and to their manager.

---

## When A Champion Stops Being A Champion

This will happen. People change jobs, take parental leave, move teams,
get promoted into roles where SAAF is no longer compatible with their
day job, or simply burn out.

The graceful exit:

1. They tell the captain (or the captain notices) they are stepping
   back.
2. The captain thanks them publicly, in writing, with their manager
   cc'd.
3. They hand over any plan, track, or recurring artefact they owned to
   a named successor — not to "the community."
4. Their name stays on the artefacts they built. Forever.
5. They are welcome back. Always.

If a former champion becomes hostile or actively undermines the
chapter, that is a different problem. Read
[pitfalls.md](pitfalls.md#2-the-disengaged-champion-who-stays-loud).
