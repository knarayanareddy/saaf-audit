# Running SAAF Hackathons Inside Your Organisation

> An internal SAAF programme — bank, audit firm, regulator, industrial
> group, public-sector body — that runs SAAF hackathons on a recurring
> cadence and contributes learnings back to the main SAAF Project.

This section is for someone whose ambition is internal: turn the
organisation's auditors into builders by running SAAF hackathons inside
your own walls, get senior backing, ship agents that improve the
organisation's own audit work, and connect those artefacts back to the
wider SAAF community.

The hackathon is the centrepiece. Everything in this section — sponsor
alignment, champion recruitment, operating cadence — exists to make
recurring internal hackathons happen and land.

## Get in touch with the main SAAF Project first

Before you go deep into the playbook, **reach out to the main SAAF
Project organisers**. A 30-minute conversation early on is the single
highest-leverage thing you can do — it saves weeks of reinventing the
wheel and connects your effort to what is already working elsewhere.

Contact:

- **Eduward van de Kamp** — co-founder of the main SAAF Project.
- **Mathijs Scholten** — co-founder of the main SAAF Project.

The fastest way to find them is via the main SAAF Project at
**[saafproject.com](https://www.saafproject.com)**.

What the conversation typically covers:

- An introduction to captains of other internal chapters who have
  walked this path inside their own organisations.
- The current versions of the plan template, the manifesto, and any
  shared infrastructure (synthetic-data sandbox, MCPs, the
  hackathon-harness website).
- A quick read on whether your specific organisation's context lines
  up with the patterns this playbook describes — and where it might
  not.
- The right professional-body endorsement path for your organisation,
  if relevant.
- **SAAF Foundation affiliation** — the formal recognition that lets
  your internal chapter use the SAAF name and logo. Affiliation is a
  conversation, not an application form; this is where it starts.

You do not need permission to run a community of auditors building AI
audit agents inside your organisation. You do need **SAAF Foundation
affiliation** to call that community an internal SAAF chapter and to
use the SAAF name and logo — the marks are owned by the Foundation
and reserved for affiliated chapters. The conversation above is how
affiliation begins; it is an offer of help, not a gatekeeping step.

## How an internal chapter relates to a regional chapter

An internal chapter is **inside one firewall**. A regional chapter is
**across many firewalls**. The differences that matter:

| Dimension | Internal chapter | Regional chapter |
|---|---|---|
| Sponsorship | Senior executive (CIA / CIO / CRO) | Professional body endorsement |
| IP / sharing | Internal first, community second | Community first |
| Real data access | Possible (with controls) | Forbidden — synthetic only |
| Champions | Inside teams | Across organisations |
| Budget | Internal cost centre | Sponsorship + member contributions |
| Cadence | Often more frequent (monthly / quarterly) | Quarterly |
| Recap audience | Senior management | All members |

If your organisation is large enough — say, 200+ auditors across
multiple jurisdictions — you may need to run *both* an internal chapter
*and* participate in a regional one. The two compose well; an internal
chapter often produces the most disciplined contributions to the
regional pool.

## Before you start

Re-read [foundations/when-to-start.md](../foundations/when-to-start.md).
The internal-specific signal: if you cannot name *one* senior executive
who has said "yes" out loud, in writing, you do not yet have an
internal chapter — you have an idea you would like to be a chapter.
Sit on it until that signal arrives.
