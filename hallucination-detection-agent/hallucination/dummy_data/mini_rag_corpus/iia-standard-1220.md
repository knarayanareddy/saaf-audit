# IIA Standard 1220 — Due Professional Care

**Standard 1220 — Due Professional Care**

*Internal auditors must apply the care and skill expected of a reasonably prudent and competent internal auditor. Due professional care does not imply infallibility.*

## 1220.A1 — In exercising due professional care, internal auditors must consider:

- The extent of work needed to achieve the engagement's objectives.
- The relative complexity, materiality, or significance of matters to which assurance procedures are applied.
- The adequacy and effectiveness of governance, risk management, and control processes.
- The probability of significant errors, fraud, non-compliance, and other exposures.
- The cost of assurance in relation to potential benefits.

## 1220.A2 — In exercising due professional care, internal auditors must consider the use of technology-based audit and other data analysis techniques.

## 1220.A3 — Internal auditors must be alert to the significant risks that might affect objectives, operations, or resources.

## Application to AI-assisted audit work

When using AI-generated content in engagements:
- Auditors must evaluate AI outputs for accuracy, completeness, and relevance before incorporating them into workpapers.
- The auditor remains responsible for the quality of the engagement even when AI tools produce intermediate outputs.
- Relying on AI output without validation breaches due professional care under Standard 1220.
- Hallucination detection is a legitimate part of the quality process for AI-assisted engagements.
